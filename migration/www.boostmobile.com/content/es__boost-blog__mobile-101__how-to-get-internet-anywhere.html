How Wi-Fi Works

Wi-Fi is the invisible technology that powers much of our daily lives. It connects all your devices-such as smartphones, tablets, and laptops-to the internet without cables.

Wi-Fi works by rapidly sending data via radio waves from a central hub (like a router) to an available device, similar to how broadcast radio works. Understanding how Wi-Fi works can help you stay connected wherever life takes you.

Wi-Fi vs. Mobile Data vs. Hotspots

While Wi-Fi, mobile data, and hotspots all keep you connected, they work differently:

  • Wi-Fi uses local networks (like your home router or public hotspots)

  • Mobile data connects directly to your carrier’s cellular network

  • Hotspots bridge the two by letting you share your mobile data or a dedicated device’s connection with other devices

This flexibility and diversity of options are what make getting Wi-Fi on the go possible, helping students, travelers and remote workers stay connected no matter where they are.

The Best Ways To Get Wi-Fi Everywhere

From mobile hotspots to public networks, there are several reliable ways to get internet anywhere, depending on your needs and location.

Mobile Hotspots

A mobile device Wi-Fi hotspot allows you to share your phone’s data connection. On most smartphones, you can activate it under “Hotspot” or “Tethering” within your device’s settings. It’s quick to set up and perfect for short-term use.

While using your smartphone as a hotspot is convenient, it has limitations. It can drain your battery fairly quickly and use a lot of data, so it's better for occasional connections rather than frequent daily use. You'll also want to ensure that your mobile plan provides hotspot access for your phone before you begin.

How To Set Up Your Mobile Hotspot

Turning your smartphone into a mobile hotspot is a quick way to share your data connection with other devices like laptops or tablets. Here's how to get started:

  1. Open Settings – On your phone, go to Settings and look for Hotspot or Tethering (names may vary by device)

  2. Enable Hotspot – Toggle the option to turn on your hotspot. Your phone will broadcast a Wi-Fi signal

  3. Set a Network Name (SSID) – Choose a recognizable name so you can identify your hotspot on other devices

  4. Create a Strong Password – Protect your connection with a secure password to prevent unauthorized use

  5. Connect Your Device – On your laptop or tablet, open Wi-Fi/network settings, select your hotspot’s name, and enter the password

Pro-tip: Keep your phone plugged in when using it as a hotspot since it can drain the battery quickly. Also, check your data plan to ensure hotspot use is an included feature.

Portable Wi-Fi Devices and Travel Routers

A portable Wi-Fi device (sometimes called a travel router or portable hotspot) provides a dedicated internet connection using a SIM card. Unlike phone tethering, it won't drain your battery. It's a great solution for frequent travelers, students, or families who need reliable internet access.

However, these types of devices often require their own monthly data plan, which can be set up when purchasing the device.

Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi is one of the easiest and cheapest ways to get connected while out and about. You'll find it in coffee shops, airports, hotels, libraries, shopping centers, and even some public transportation hubs. For travelers or students, it can be a quick solution to check email, browse the web, or download files.

However, while free public Wi-Fi is convenient, it does come with limitations and security concerns. Speeds can vary depending on how many people are connected, and networks may not always be reliable for streaming, video calls, or heavy work tasks. More importantly, public networks are often unsecured, which can put your personal data at risk.

Tips to Stay Safe While on Public Wi-Fi

  • Use a VPN (Virtual Private Network) to encrypt your connection
  • Avoid logging into sensitive accounts like banking apps
  • Always verify that the Wi-Fi network you’re connecting to is legitimate and provided by the business

Public Wi-Fi can be a lifesaver when looking for a quick way to get online, but for regular, secure use, options like a Boost Mobile’s cell phone hotspot plans or a portable Wi-Fi router are better long-term solutions.

Internet Options in Remote Areas

For truly remote areas where there is neither a mobile network connection nor available public Wi-Fi, satellite internet (like Starlink or Hughesnet) is going to be your best option. While it can be one of the most expensive ways to connect on our list, it delivers coverage in places traditional networks can’t reach.

These types of satellite internet options usually require a paid monthly plan, often with high-speed data caps. However, if you're planning to travel to remote areas, camp in the backcountry or sail the open sea, satellite internet will likely be the only way to stay online.

Which Option is Best For Your Needs?

The best way to connect to Wi-Fi while out on the town will depend on where you are traveling, your budget, and how much data you will need to browse online. Comparing speed, cost and the convenience to connect will help you make an informed decision on which option will work for you.
