Why the Solo Plan Math Finally Makes Sense

If you've historically stuck with a major carrier as a single user, you're likely paying a premium for being on a plan with only one line. The best new customer deals are often for 2+ lines, with shrinking savings for the solo saver. Navigating these deals and finding which carrier is best can be easier than you think with a simple overview of the factors that go into the "value" of a phone plan.

The Postpaid Trap

Legacy carriers build their pricing models around multi-line accounts. A flagship plan that costs a reasonable $45 to $55 per line for a family of four can easily jump to $90 or more when you only need one line. You're essentially paying retail prices while families get wholesale rates.

The Prepaid Advantage

Modern prepaid carriers and MVNOs (Mobile Virtual Network Operators) have democratized access to the exact same premium 5G towers. Instead of scaling prices based on how many people sign up, companies like Boost Mobile offer flat-rate pricing. One line gets an amazing deal right out of the gate.

No Hidden Surcharges

Most of the top-tier solo plans bake their taxes and regulatory fees directly into the sticker price. When a legacy carrier advertises an $80 plan, you often see a bill for $92. When Boost advertises $25, that's the base rate you pay.

Deep Dive: The Best Phone Plans for One

The Champion: Boost Mobile

Right now, Boost Mobile is offering the most aggressive and consumer-friendly single-line deal in the industry. Their Unlimited plan is a flat $25 a month, but the real magic is the lifetime price lock.

As long as you keep your line active, your bill will never increase. You get unlimited talk, text, and 30GB of premium 5G data. To sweeten the pot, they are currently running a new customer promotion where your first three months cost just $10 a month, solo plans included. You get premium network access without the 12-month lock-in period required by other carriers.

Visible

Owned by Verizon, Visible offers a brilliantly simple proposition: $25/mo for unlimited data on Verizon's network. It's entirely digital and you handle everything through their app. If you live in an area where Verizon dominates the coverage map and you use massive amounts of data, this is a strong contender, though data on their most affordable plan may be deprioritized.

Mint Mobile

Mint is famous for its bulk-pricing model. To get their absolute best rates, like an effective $15/mo for their unlimited plan, you need to pay for 12 months of service upfront. If you have the cash on hand to prepay for a year, it's a stellar deal on the T-Mobile network.

Google Fi

For the frequent international traveler, Google Fi is unmatched. Their Flexible plan ($20/mo plus $10 per GB) is great if you use almost no data, but the Unlimited Standard at $50/mo is the true draw. It includes high-speed data and texting in over 200 countries seamlessly, no travel passes or local SIM cards required.
