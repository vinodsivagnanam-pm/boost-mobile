How to Switch Carriers in Four Easy Steps

Step 1: Review Your Current Contract

Before making the leap, take a few minutes to understand where you stand with your current provider.

  • Check for fees: Some carriers charge Early Termination Fees (ETFs) if you’re still under contract.

  • Device contract: If you are financing your phone, you’ll likely need to pay off the remaining balance before leaving your current network.

  • Unlock your phone: To bring your own phone to a new network, the device must be unlocked. Check your current carrier’s unlocking policies. Boost makes it easy to see if you're unlocked using your phone’s IMEI number and answering a few questions.

Step 2: Confirm Device Compatibility

If you’re planning to bring your own device, make sure it’s compatible with Boost coverage.

  • Check your IMEI: Use an online IMEI checker to confirm your specific phone model is compatible with the new network infrastructure.

  • SIM card or eSIM: Determine if your phone uses a traditional SIM or eSIM, and we’ll get you set up with whichever works for your device.

Step 4: Activate Your Service & Keep Your Number

You're almost done. Just activate and start saving.

What to Consider When Switching Phone Carriers

Early Termination Fees

If you are currently under contract, review your carrier’s ETF policy. In many cases, the long-term monthly savings of switching will outweigh the one-time cost of a final bill, but it is important to calculate this upfront.

Device Financing & New Phone Cost

If your phone is not entirely paid off, your current carrier will bill you for the remaining balance upon cancellation. You can either pay this off to unlock and keep your phone, or trade it in and take advantage of new customer device promotions.

Connecting Other Devices

If you need connectivity for tablets, smartwatches, or mobile hotspots, look for carriers that offer affordable add-on lines. BYOD data plans for secondary devices can often be added for as little as $10/month.

Price Guarantees

Many low-cost phone plans feature introductory pricing that quietly increases after a few months. With Boost Mobile, our $25/month unlimited plan comes with a price guarantee. As long as your account remains active and in good standing, your base monthly rate will not increase.

How to Keep Your Number When Switching Carriers

Keeping your current phone number (a process known as "porting") is a common part of switching carriers, especially if you plan to bring your own phone. To ensure a seamless transfer, follow these steps:

  1. Do not cancel your current service. Your number must be active to be transferred.

  2. Request a Transfer PIN. Contact your current carrier to request a specific Number Transfer PIN (this is different from your regular security PIN).

  3. Provide the details to your new carrier. Give your new provider your account number, billing zip code, and Transfer PIN. Once they initiate the port, your old service will automatically cancel the line once the transfer is complete.

Things To Avoid When Switching Carriers

Switching phone carriers is simple, but there are a few common mistakes that can cost you time and money. Here’s what to watch out for:

1. Canceling Your Old Plan Too Early

Don’t cancel your current service before transferring your number. If you do, you risk losing your number permanently. Always let the new carrier handle the porting process.

2. Forgetting to Unlock Your Phone

Your phone must be unlocked to work on a new network. If it’s still tied to your current carrier, you won’t be able to activate it with Boost.

3. Not Checking Compatibility

Not all phones work on every network. Always use an IMEI checker to confirm your device is compatible before switching.

4. Ignoring Final Bills or Fees

Some carriers charge final bills, remaining device payments, or Early Termination Fees. Knowing what you owe upfront helps you avoid surprises.

5. Skipping the Data Backup

Before switching, back up your contacts, photos, and important files. While switching carriers doesn’t usually erase your data, it’s better to be safe.

6. Entering Incorrect Account Info

When transferring your number, your account number and PIN must match exactly. Even a small error can delay the process.
