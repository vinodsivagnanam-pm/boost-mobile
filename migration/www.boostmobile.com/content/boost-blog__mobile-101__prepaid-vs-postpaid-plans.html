How Do Prepaid and Postpaid Plans Work?

Making the right choice between prepaid vs postpaid mobile plans begins with understanding how each works. From how and when you pay, to the specific features you get, the structure of these plans shape how you use your phone and data.

How Prepaid Plans Work

Prepaid plans are designed to meet unique data, talk, and texting needs, specifically focused on flexibility. The specifics of your mobile phone plan are determined when you sign up, with fixed prices due at the beginning of each month.

This type of cell phone plan can help keep your monthly bills predictable and flexible, so you’re never surprised by overage fees or unexpected charges. You select your data caps, talk and text limits, and how many devices will be on the plan.

Step-by-Step Guide to Prepaid Plans

  1. Choose a plan – You pick how much talk, text, and data you want. With Boost, all prepaid options come with unlimited talk and text, so you just select how much premium data and which features you want included with your plan.
  2. Pay before you use – You pay at the start of the month or even just for a week, you choose exactly what you need.
  3. Use your phone – You’re ready to go! Call your mom back, text your friends, and check your socials, — your phone plan is good to go.
  4. Run out? Add more! – Used more data or talking minutes than expected this month? Add more anytime. Luckily, many of Boost’s prepaid plans offer unlimited talk and text, so you’ll never have to worry about running out with our Unlimited plans.

How Postpaid Plans Work

When it comes to postpaid plans, you use your phone service throughout the month and then pay for it afterward, like a utility bill. These plans often require a credit check and may require a contract to get started.

Luckily, postpaid mobile plan options typically include extra perks, like free or discounted phones when you sign up, unlimited data, and family plan discounts. Postpaid perks often make them a good fit for people who want premium features and don’t mind a monthly commitment.

Step-by-Step Guide to Postpaid Plans

  1. Choose a plan – Pick the plan that fits your needs, like unlimited talk, text, and mobile data.
  2. Start using your phone – No upfront payment required, just start talking, texting, or streaming right away.
  3. Get your bill – At the end of the month, you get a bill for the service you used. This is most often an amount you will select when creating your plan.
  4. Pay after use – You pay the bill at the end of the month. Just like paying your power bill, which may be adjusted to match your use. The specifics of your unique plan will determine if your bill changes at all from month to month.

Key Differences Between Prepaid and Postpaid Plans

When picking the right plan for you, understanding these few key distinctions can help you decide if a prepaid or contract phone plan is a better fit. From billing flexibility to device options, each plan type has unique advantages and drawbacks.

Here’s how to choose between prepaid and postpaid based on the biggest differences between the two.

Cost and Billing Flexibility

When comparing prepaid or postpaid plans, it's worth noting that prepaid gives you more control over your monthly costs. With no overage fees or surprise charges, Boost Mobile’s flat-rate prepaid plans make managing your monthly budget a piece of cake.

On the other hand, postpaid plans can offer flexibility when it comes to adding multiple lines to a single plan, a great option for big families.

Credit Checks and Contracts

Prepaid plans don’t require a credit check or lengthy contracts, making them ideal for those who want freedom and privacy, with no strings attached.

Many postpaid cell phone plans also have contract-free options, allowing you to cancel or change service at any time. However, if you would like perks like annual device upgrades or unlimited global talk and text, a contract is often required.

Features and Device Options

Think a prepaid phone means limited features or slow speeds? Think again. Today’s prepaid plans have evolved a good deal from plans of the past, offering premium perks like lightning fast 5G coverage and unlimited data.

When Prepaid Plans Might Be a Better Fit

Prepaid phone plans have come a long way since the days of buying a cheap 20-minute phone at a convenience store. They’re now a smart, flexible choice for anyone looking to save money without sacrificing features. Now available on all the same smartphones you’d expect to see on any contract phone plan.

No Surprises, No Long-Term Commitments

Say goodbye to hidden fees, credit checks, and multi-year contracts. With prepaid plans, you get the freedom to pay as you go and end service anytime you want, no strings attached. You keep full control over every part of your plan, from the phone to the data limits.

Bring Your Own Phone (BYOP)

With Boost Mobile’s BYOP plans, you won’t need to spend money on a new device to start service. It’s as easy as signing up online or visiting an in-person Boost Mobile location, then just pop in a SIM card and you’re all set.

When Postpaid Plans Might Be a Better Fit

If you’re someone who uses a lot of data, wants the best deals on new phones, or needs to manage multiple lines on one account, a postpaid plan will be the perfect fit. These plans often come with added perks like free annual phone upgrades, loyalty rewards, and unlimited worldwide service options all at one predictable monthly price.

When You Need Unlimited Everything

If you stream, scroll, and share nonstop, postpaid offers unlimited data plans prepaid may not always be able to match. You won’t have to worry about data caps or throttling, even with multiple devices and people on the same plan.

Family Plans and Shared Lines

Planning to add the whole family to your mobile plan? Postpaid plans often have great deals to support multiple lines on one account, giving you shared data, consolidated billing, and extra savings. It's a convenient option for households that need connectivity without complication.

FAQs

What is a prepaid phone plan?

A prepaid phone plan means you pay for your talk, text, and data upfront, before using the service. There are no monthly bills or surprise charges, just straightforward mobile plan control without the long-term commitments.

Are prepaid phones cheaper than postpaid?

Yes, prepaid plans are often more affordable than postpaid ones. However, the specifics will depend upon exactly what you want out of your plan. If you want a discounted new phone, a postpaid plan may work out to be cheaper in the long run when factoring in device deals and annual upgrades.

If you already have a phone and want to get it set up ASAP, Boost Mobile’s BYOP plans will be the cheapest option available.

How much does a prepaid phone cost?

The cost of your prepaid phone will vary depending on what type of device you are on and the mobile plan you choose.

You can bring your own phone or buy a prepaid phone online or in-person, with plans often starting around $10–$30 per month.

Is a prepaid or postpaid phone plan better for students?

If your parents already have a postpaid phone plan set up, adding a line onto theirs would likely be the best option for students. If you plan to set up the plan on your own, prepaid will often be the cheapest option for students. These plans will help you maintain control of a limited budget and lower monthly costs, all without the need for any credit checks (perfect for students with low or no credit).

Is a prepaid or postpaid phone plan better for families?

That depends on your family's unique needs. Postpaid plans often support multiple lines and shared data, which can be the most cost efficient option when adding more than 2 lines to the plan. However, prepaid plans may be more cost-effective for families that want to set individual budgets or avoid overage fees.

Does prepaid include 5G?

Yes! Boost Mobile’s prepaid plans include access to lightning-fast 5G speeds on compatible phones, so you don’t have to sacrifice performance for prepaid prices. Premium data may have limits on many prepaid phone plans, which can be slowed after hitting your monthly limit.
